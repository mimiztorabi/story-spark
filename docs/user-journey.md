# User Journey

## Persona

**Maya, a busy caregiver** — Maya has ten minutes before bedtime and wants an easy, screen-light activity. She is curious about AI but does not want to hand over judgment about what her child sees.

## Journey map

| Stage | Maya’s need | Product response | Risk addressed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arrive | “What is this?” | A plain-language portfolio and simulation label. | Avoids implying a live AI service. |
| Choose | “Make it feel relevant.” | Three bounded story ingredients. | Limits unexpected subject matter. |
| Generate | “Give me a starting point.” | A short deterministic outline. | Avoids opaque or unreviewed output. |
| Review | “Is this right for my child?” | An adult-review prompt beside the result. | Reinforces caregiver agency. |
| Adapt | “I want a different idea.” | Change selections and try again. | Avoids persistence or data collection. |

## Moment that matters

The highest-trust moment is immediately after the idea appears. The caregiver should understand that the suggestion is a draft—not an authority—and feel invited to change it before sharing.
